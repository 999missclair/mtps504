STAYING SAFE

If something goes wrong

Nothing here gets you in trouble. Read it once now so you know where to look later.

1 · You see something you didn't want to see

You are searching for pictures. Something comes up that is upsetting, rude, or just wrong. This is not your fault and you are not in trouble.

  1. Turn the screen away or close the tab.
  2. Tell your teacher. Out loud, or quietly at the desk — either is fine.
  3. That's it. You go back to work.

You will never be in trouble for telling a teacher what you saw. The only thing that causes a problem is showing it to other people first.

Want to avoid it altogether? Use the class image bank. Every picture in there has already been checked.

2 · What goes on the class board

When you post your strip, you use your first name and your table number. Not your surname. Not your school. Not where you live.

Your strip is visible to your class. It is not visible to the whole internet, and your full name is never attached to it.

3 · Commenting on someone else's strip

You get given two strips to comment on, so nobody's work sits there with nothing under it. Every comment says three things:

  • one shot you noticed
  • where you think the joke lands
  • one what if

You are commenting on the frames, not on the person who made them. If a comment on your strip isn't kind, tell your teacher. Same rule as above — you are not in trouble.

4 · What this site knows about you

There is no login or cookies. Your route choice, rolled story and comic-in-progress are kept only on this device, so they can follow you between pages. GIF and AI helpers send what you type to the class tool to work. Refreshing or clearing the device can remove your draft.

Your work also lives in your Google Drive and on the class wall, which is why we post before the bell.

? The short version

  • See something bad? Close it, tell the teacher. No trouble.
  • First name and table number only.
  • Comment on the frames, not the person.
  • Your story and comic stay on this device. Post before the bell.

Who to tell

Who to tell: Tell your teacher — in person, or a quick message. If it's urgent and you can't reach them, tell any teacher you trust.

  • No account, no cookies. Your story and comic stay on this device.
  • Your teacher is in the room, and quicker than this page.
