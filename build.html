Producing & implementing

4 Build

You’ve got a plan. Now build the four frames.

Four images, four frames, one strip. You find your pictures first, because that is the part that takes the time. Then you open your own copy of the template, drop them in, write the captions and the credit lines, and download the whole thing as one picture. The deck lives in your Google Drive. The finished strip lives on the class gallery. Nothing lives here.

Before you start

  1. 1 Nothing you make today is saved on this laptop.
  2. 2 Your slide lives in your Google Drive. Your finished picture lives on the class Padlet.
  3. 3 Download your images first, build second, and post before the bell.

Already started?

Don’t take a second copy. Go and find the one you already have.

  1. slides.google.com Look under Recent. Yours is the one with your name in it.
  2. drive.google.com Search FourFrames. Still nothing? You may have been working in the view-only master, which doesn’t save. Ask for the link and start a fresh copy.

The eight steps, in order

Do them in this order. Steps 3 and 8 are the two that catch everybody.

  1. Find your four images One per frame. Use the three sources below — they tell you the licence.
  2. Download each one It lands in your Downloads folder. Keep the tab open so you can copy the credit.
  3. Open your copy of the template First time: press the big button and choose Make a copy. After that: Drive, or the Classroom assignment. Show me →
  4. Rename it FourFrames_8VA_Surname_FirstName. Do this now, not at the end.
  5. Insert your images Insert → Image → Upload from computer, or drag the file straight from Downloads onto the slide.
  6. Arrange the four frames Setup, escalation, escalation, punchline — the order you planned. Drag to move, corner handles to resize.
  7. Write your captions and credits A caption under each frame, and one credit line per image in the box at the bottom.
  8. ↩ You are here Download it as a PNG, then post it File → Download → PNG image. It only downloads the slide you are looking at. Then open the class gallery and post it with your four credit lines. Show me →

        ? Stuck? Try this

        • Can’t find a picture of your character? Search the place instead. An empty park does the job a lonely greyhound won’t, and the caption carries the rest.
        • Image came in the size of a house? Click it once, then drag a corner handle. Corners keep the shape. Side handles squash it.
        • Nothing is happening in your strip? Change the camera, not the story. Frame 1 wide, frames 2 and 3 closer, frame 4 wide again.

        What good looks like

        Read your strip back. Tick everything that’s true.

        • My four frames tell one story, in order.
        • Frame 4 is the funny one — the surprise lands last.
        • I’ve used at least two different shot types.
        • Something repeats across my frames, and one thing changes at the end.
        • Each frame has one line I could put under the picture.
        • I can name the licence of every image I’m planning to use.

        Anything you didn’t tick is your next job. Nobody ticks all six first go.

        Going further

        Six frames instead of four, or a two-page strip. Same rules, more room for the joke to build — and one more credit line for every image you add.
