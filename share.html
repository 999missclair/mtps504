Evaluating

5 Share

Is it finished, and how would you know?

Nobody hands you the marking key today — the class builds it, and then you hold your own strip against it. You are marked here on what you notice, not on how funny you are.

1 · Build the criteria

Fifteen minutes, out loud, as a class. You’ve all made one now, so you know what worked.

  1. Say one thing a good four-frame strip does One each. Not “it’s funny” — say what it does that makes it funny.
  2. Put the repeats together Half the class will say the same three things in different words. Those are the ones that matter.
  3. Agree on six Six, in your words, on the board. Every rule on the list must be checkable by looking at the strip. If you can’t see it, it’s off the list.

The six we started from

These are the ones from the planner. They’re the starting list, not the final one — cross out what the class doesn’t agree with and put your own words in.

  • My four frames tell one story, in order.
  • Frame 4 is the funny one — the surprise lands last.
  • I’ve used at least two different shot types.
  • Something repeats across my frames, and one thing changes at the end.
  • Each frame has one line I could put under the picture.
  • I can name the licence of every image I’m planning to use.

The last one isn’t negotiable. The other five are.

Then mark your own, honestly

Open your strip next to the six. For each one: yes, nearly, or not yet. Write one line under the ones that aren’t a yes saying what you’d change.

“Not yet” is the useful answer. Ticked all six? Swap with the person next to you and see if they agree. They usually find the seventh thing.

One more line, and it’s the last one: what did the roll do for you — did the cards you didn’t choose matter?

2 · Post your strip

The gallery is where this project actually finishes. A PNG in a Downloads folder is not handed in.

  1. Post the PNG Not the Slides file. The picture you downloaded from File → Download → PNG image.
  2. Title it with your rolled brief The same line you copied on 2 Roll, dots and all: A ceramic teapot · The lights go out · They’ve fallen for the sculpture opposite
  3. Paste your four credit lines underneath One per frame: Title — Creator — Source — Licence
  4. Post as your first name and table number No surnames. Sam T4 is perfect.

To finish it: build the board, open Share → Embed in your blog or website, and paste the iframe over this box. Comments must be on or step 3 has nowhere to happen. Test it once in a private window, logged out, on a school laptop.

3 · Comment on two others

Comment on the two posts after yours in the grid — wrap around to the start if yours is near the end. Use the criteria the class just built — that’s what they’re for.

What a comment has to have

  1. Name a shot Establishing, mid or close-up — say which one is doing the most work, and what it does. “The close-up in frame 3 makes the crack look massive.”
  2. Say where the joke lands Which frame, and what tipped it. “It lands in 4, because the tour group turns up out of nowhere.”
  3. Offer one “what if…” One idea, not a list. “What if frame 1 was wider, so the gap looked bigger?” They can ignore it.

Not “that’s good”. Not “lol”. A comment that could be pasted under any strip in the room isn’t about that strip.

Then read the two you got

Somebody has just told you what your strip does. Pick one thing out of your two comments that you’d actually change, and write a line under your own post saying what it is and why.

You don’t have to agree with them. Saying why you didn’t is a better answer than changing something you don’t believe in.

You’re done ✓

Rolled it, planned it, built it, credited it, posted it, and told two other people what their strip does. That’s the whole design process, start to finish.

Stuck? Try this

  • Can’t think of a criterion? Look at the strip you liked most on the board and finish this sentence: “the reason that one works is…” That’s a criterion.
  • Padlet won’t take your post? It’s probably asking for a nickname — first name only. Or the file is the Slides deck, not the PNG.
  • Don’t know what to comment? Use the three parts above in order. Shot, frame, what if. Three sentences and you’re done.

What good looks like

Before you call it finished, tick everything that’s true.

  • My four frames tell one story, in order.
  • Frame 4 is the funny one — the surprise lands last.
  • I’ve used at least two different shot types.
  • Something repeats across my frames, and one thing changes at the end.
  • Each frame has one line I could put under the picture.
  • I can name the licence of every image I’m planning to use.

Anything you didn’t tick is your next job. Nobody ticks all six first go.

Going further

Find the other strips that rolled the same character as you. Same card, completely different stories — write two lines on what each maker decided that you didn’t.
