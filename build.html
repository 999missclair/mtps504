Build your comic

This is where you make your comic. It all happens in the Comic Builder — nothing to download, no template to copy.

Open the Comic Builder →

  1. Drop your four pictures into the four frames.
  2. Keep the final story wordless. No character dialogue. A short accessibility caption and your credit lines are allowed.
  3. Arrange the frames set-up · build-up · build-up · funny ending — the order you planned.
  4. Save (export) your comic as an image.

Quick check: where can this picture go?

Three minutes. Not marked — this one’s just so you don’t get caught out.

This is a bonus support activity. It isn’t one of the three marked ones.

Can I use this in my comic? and can I put it where the whole internet can see it? are different questions — the second is stricter. Sort these five and you’ll feel it.

        0 of 5 placed.

        Keyboard: press Enter or Space to pick an image up, Tab to a bin, then Enter or Space to put it down. Escape puts it back.

        The middle two were the interesting ones

        Nothing moved between bins because the picture changed. It moved because the destination changed. Why? See the rule below.

        Going further: you built your strip from a CC BY-SA image. Someone wants to use your strip. What are they allowed to do with it?

        ! Anything you post publicly uses an image whose licence you can name.

        In class, you have more room than that. Studying artworks and making a joke about one is covered. Putting somebody else’s picture where the whole internet can reach it is not — so when it goes on the board, it goes with an open licence.

        Every frame gets one credit line, in this order:

        Title — Creator — Source — Licence
        Under the Wave off Kanagawa (Kanagawa oki nami ura), also known as The Great Wave — Katsushika Hokusai — The Met Open Access — CC0

        Four frames, four credit lines — on your comic and in your class-wall post, whatever the licence says.

        Watch out for CC BY-SA. “Share-alike” means anyone is allowed to reuse your finished strip the same free way. That’s fine for a class wall — just know you’ve said yes to it.

        What one looks like finished

        1. Set-up — wide shot, lots of space between them.
        2. Build-up — lights out, closer in.
        3. Build-up — closer still, the gap looks smaller.
        4. Funny ending — lights on, wide again, a tour group parked between them.

        The story has no character dialogue. The short captions and credit lines help other people understand and reuse it properly.

        Describe one panel for access

        Alt text tells someone who cannot see the picture what is in it. Do one panel at a time. The helper drafts a sentence, then you check and fix it — the AI is your assistant, not the boss.

        Helper asleep? Write one plain sentence on what the panel shows — that is all alt text is.

        Post it before the bell

        A comic you exported but didn’t post is not handed in — the laptop gets wiped.

        1. Open the class wall New post, upload the image you exported from the Comic Builder.
        2. Paste your four credit lines One per frame, in the post, in the order the frames run.
        3. Post as your first name and table number No surnames. Sam T4 is perfect.

        Go to 5 Share to post it to the class wall.

        Optional: compare a finished comic with an AI version

        Once some comics are finished, Clair runs two or three (with permission) through an AI image tool live, on the projector, and the class discusses what changed.

        This is a teacher-run demonstration, not a student activity. No AI tool is something you use yourself here.

        Five questions, worked through as a class

        1. What did the AI version do better? Be specific — name the element or principle.
        2. What did it lose? Usually: the joke, the timing, and the specificity of the choices.
        3. The AI version had no constraint. Yours had a dice roll. Which produced the better idea, and why?
        4. Whose work did the model learn from to make this — were they asked?
        5. If you submitted the AI version, what exactly would you be claiming you made?

        Before any comic goes near the tool: the title block is stripped, and the tool is named out loud.

        Stuck? Try this

        • Can’t find a picture of your character? Search the place instead. You won’t find a photo of a lonely greyhound. You will find an empty park. Put the greyhound in the caption.
        • Image came in the size of a house? Click it once, then drag a corner handle. Corners keep the shape. Side handles squash it.
        • Nothing is happening in your strip? Change the camera, not the story. Frame 1 wide, frames 2 and 3 closer, frame 4 wide again.

        What good looks like

        Read your strip back. Tick everything that’s true.

        • My four frames tell one story, in order.
        • Frame 4 is the funny one — the surprise lands last.
        • I’ve used at least two different shot types.
        • Something repeats across my frames, and one thing changes at the end.
        • Each frame has one line I could put under the picture.
        • I can name the licence of every image I’m planning to use.

        Anything you didn’t tick is your next job. Nobody ticks all six first go.

        Going further

        Six frames instead of four, or a two-page strip. Same rules, more room for the joke to build — and one more credit line for every image you add.
