Evaluating

5 Share

Is it finished, and how would you know?

Last stop: check your strip before it joins the class wall. You’re marked on what you notice — not on how funny it is.

1 · Build the checklist

Fifteen minutes, out loud, as a class. You’ve all made one now, so you know what worked.

Before final submission: in the Comic Builder, fill the four credit slots, press Copy all four credits, then download your comic. Keep this page open, then paste those lines underneath your comic.

  1. Say one thing a good four-frame strip does One each. Not “it’s funny” — say what it does that makes it funny.
  2. Put the repeats together Half the class will say the same three things in different words. Those are the ones that matter.
  3. Agree on six Six, in your words, on the board. Every rule on the list must be checkable by looking at the strip. If you can’t see it, it’s off the list.

The six we started from

These are the ones from the planner — the starting list, not the final one. Cross out what the class doesn’t agree with and put your own words in. See the rail for the full six. You can’t drop the last one; the other five you can change.

Then mark your own, honestly

Open your strip next to the six. For each one: yes, nearly, or not yet. Write one line under the ones that aren’t a yes saying what you’d change.

“Not yet” is the useful answer. Swap with your neighbour and see if they agree — they usually spot one more thing.

One more line, and it’s the last one: what did the roll do for you — did the cards you didn’t choose matter?

Alt text before it goes up

Before your comic hits the class wall, give it alt text so everyone can read it. Describe a panel in your own words; the helper drafts it, then you check and fix it.

Sidekick taking five? Write one plain sentence on what the panel shows.

5 · Share your finished comic

Your comic belongs in the Final submission Padlet. Post the picture, your four credits, and one line about something you learned while making it.

First: download your comic

  1. 1 Return to the Comic Builder and use Download my comic.
  2. 2 Come back here. The Final submission Padlet will open.

Return to Comic Builder →

Post your finished comic

  1. Post your comic image The picture you saved with the Comic Builder’s Download my comic button.
  2. Title it with your rolled brief. Use your fictional character name and table code. Never use the name of a student, teacher, friend or real person.
  3. Paste your four copied credit lines underneath One per frame: title, creator, source and licence.
  4. Add your comic description and one evaluation note Paste the description from the Builder, then finish: I learned my sequence or shot choice worked because…

3 · Comment on two others

Comment on the two posts after yours in the grid — wrap around to the start if yours is near the end. Use the checklist the class just built — that’s what it’s for.

What a comment has to have

  1. Name a shot The wide (establishing) shot, mid or close-up — say which one is doing the most work, and what it does. “The close-up in frame 3 makes the crack look massive.”
  2. Say where the joke lands Which frame, and what tipped it. “It lands in 4, because the tour group turns up out of nowhere.”
  3. Offer one “what if…” One idea, not a list. “What if frame 1 was wider, so the gap looked bigger?”

Not “that’s good”. Not “lol”.

Then read the two you got

Somebody has just told you what your strip does. Pick one thing out of your two comments that you’d actually change, and write a line under your own post saying what it is and why.

You don’t have to agree with them — just say why.

You’re done ✓

Rolled it, planned it, built it, credited it, posted it, commented on two others. Done.

Your finished comic is in the Final submission Padlet in step 2 — that’s the whole project, handed in.

Stuck? Try this

  • Can’t think of one? Look at the strip you liked most on the board and finish this sentence: “the reason that one works is…” That’s a checklist line.
  • Padlet won’t take your post? Use your fictional character name and table code. Never use the name of a student, teacher, friend or real person. Or check you posted the image from the Comic Builder’s Download my comic button.
  • Don’t know what to comment? Use the three parts above in order. Shot, frame, what if. Three sentences and you’re done.

What good looks like

Before you call it finished, tick everything that’s true.

  • My four frames tell one story, in order.
  • Frame 4 is the funny one — the surprise lands last.
  • I’ve used at least two different shot types.
  • Something repeats across my frames, and one thing changes at the end.
  • Each frame has one line I could put under the picture.
  • I can name the licence of every image I’m planning to use.

Anything you didn’t tick is your next job. Nobody ticks all six first go.

Going further

Find the other strips that rolled the same character as you. Same card, completely different stories — write two lines on what each maker decided that you didn’t.

  • No account, no cookies. Your story and comic stay on this device.
  • Your teacher is in the room, and quicker than this page.
