Project overview

The Brief

What are you making, and how long have you got?

The project

Four pictures, no words, one joke. You won’t draw a thing — you’ll hunt down photos you’re allowed to use and arrange them so a story reads left to right, top to bottom.

The whole skill is telling that story inside four fixed frames: what you show, what you hold back, and how you land the surprise on the last one. Get that right and anyone can follow your joke without a single word to help them — and that’s the point of the whole thing.

What you’re making

Four photos, no drawing, one joke on frame four. Roll for your idea, plan it, build it in Slides, post it.

Why four frames?

  1. Set it upShow us who, where, and what’s normal.
  2. Make it worseSomething changes. Get the camera closer.
  3. Make it worse againCloser still. Now we’re worried.
  4. Land the punchlineNot what we expected. If it needs explaining, it isn’t the punchline yet.

What “finished” means

  • Square, four frames, in order.
  • Caption under each frame.
  • Credit line for every image.
  • Frame 4 is the punchline.

How you hand it in

  1. 1 Download your slide as a PNG.
  2. 2 Post that PNG to the class gallery.
  3. 3 Do it before the bell — the laptop wipes itself.

What good looks like

Read your four lines back. Tick everything that’s true.

  • My four frames tell one story, in order.
  • Frame 4 is the funny one — the surprise lands last.
  • I’ve used at least two different shot types.
  • Something repeats across my frames, and one thing changes at the end.
  • Each frame has one line I could put under the picture.
  • I can name the licence of every image I’m planning to use.

Anything you didn’t tick is your next job. Nobody ticks all six first go.
