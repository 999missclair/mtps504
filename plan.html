2 · Plan your four beats

Your rolled story is up the top. Now give it four beats — one line each. Jot one line per frame — on paper or in your head — then go build.

One line per frame

  1. Frame 1 — Set-upWho, where, what’s normal.
  2. Frame 2 — Build-upThe problem shows up.
  3. Frame 3 — Build-upIt gets worse, or it turns.
  4. Frame 4 — PunchlineThe payoff. The funny one lands last.

Jot each line on paper or in your head. When you’ve got four lines, you’re ready to build.

Going further

Other shapes: the joke in threes (two the same, then a surprise), the swap (the small one ends up on top), or the silent frame (no caption on frame 3). Pick the shape that suits your roll.

Doing six frames instead of four? Use your four beats for the main moments and slot the two extra in wherever they help.

Stuck? Try this

  • Can’t remember your brief? It’s on the class board from last lesson, under your first name and table number.
  • Stuck on frame 3? It’s the frame that makes the reader think they know what happens next. They should be wrong.
  • Writing too much? Write what we see, in one line, like a caption.
  • Doing the two-frame version? Fill in frame 1 and frame 4 and write two-frame version in the frame 2 box. That’s a finished plan.

What good looks like

Read your four lines back. Tick everything that’s true.

  • My four frames tell one story, in order.
  • Frame 4 is the funny one — the surprise lands last.
  • I’ve used at least two different shot types.
  • Something repeats across my frames, and one thing changes at the end.
  • Each frame has one line I could put under the picture.
  • I can name the licence of every image I’m planning to use.

Anything you didn’t tick is your next job. Nobody ticks all six first go.
