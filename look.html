              Going further

              These six panels are one story.

              1. Put them in an order that works. You can only use four. Which two do you cut, and what does the reader have to work out on their own?
              2. Which frame is doing the punchline’s job? How can you tell without being told?

              Nothing to type. Say it to the person next to you, or to me when I get to your table.

              Stuck? Try this

              • Not sure where to begin? Start with the picture that makes you ask a question.
              • Stuck between two pictures? Put the more unusual one in frame 3.
              • Use Read the story to see one possible reading of your own four-picture order.

              What good looks like

              Read your four lines back. Tick everything that’s true.

              • My four frames tell one story, in order.
              • Frame 4 is the funny one — the surprise lands last.
              • Frame 3 changes the situation, and frame 4 makes me re-think it.
              • I can explain why I left two pictures out.
              • Each frame has one line I could put under the picture.
              • I can name the licence of every image I’m planning to use.

              Anything you didn’t tick is your next job. Nobody ticks all six first go.
